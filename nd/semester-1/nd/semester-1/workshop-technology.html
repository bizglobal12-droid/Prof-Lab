MEC 113: Workshop Technology & Practice I

Module 1: Safety Regulations, Hand Tools, and Bench Work

1.1 Industrial Workshop Safety & PPE

Mechanical workshop practice requires strict adherence to safety codes to prevent mechanical injuries, chemical exposures, and fire hazards during manufacturing operations.

Classification of Workshop Fires

  • Class A: Fires involving solid combustible materials like wood, paper, and textiles. (Extinguisher: Water / Foam)
  • Class B: Flammable liquids such as oils, gasoline, and paints. (Extinguisher: Carbon Dioxide / Dry Powder)
  • Class C: Gas fires involving methane, propane, or butane. (Extinguisher: Dry Chemical Powder)
  • Class D: Combustible metals like magnesium and aluminum swarf. (Extinguisher: Specialized Dry Powder)
Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Essentials

Steel-toe safety boots, heavy-duty leather gloves, protective eye goggles (BS EN 166 compliant), and fitted shop aprons are mandatory in all machining zones.

1.2 Bench Work & Hand Threading

Bench fitting forms the foundation of mechanical fabrication, involving marking-out, hacksawing, precision filing, tapping, and thread cutting.

Internal & External Screw Threading

Internal threads are cut into drilled holes using hand taps, while external threads on cylindrical shafts are cut using threading dies.

$$\text{Tap Drill Diameter (TDD)} = D - P$$

Where $D$ is the nominal major thread diameter (mm) and $P$ is the thread pitch (mm).
