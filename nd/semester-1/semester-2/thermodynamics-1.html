MEC 121: Thermodynamics I

Module 1: First Law Foundations, Non-Flow and Steady-Flow Energy Equations

1.1 First Law for a Closed System (NFEE)

The First Law of Thermodynamics expresses the conservation of energy principle. For a closed system undergoing a thermodynamic cycle or process, net heat interaction equals net work done plus internal energy change.

Non-Flow Energy Equation (NFEE)

$$Q = \Delta U + W$$

Where $Q$ is heat added to the system ($\text{kJ}$), $\Delta U$ is internal energy change ($U_2 - U_1$), and $W$ is boundary work done ($\text{kJ}$).

Polytropic Process Boundary Work

$$W = \int_{1}^{2} P \, dV = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{n - 1}$$

1.2 Steady Flow Energy Equation (SFEE)

For open control volumes (turbines, pumps, nozzles, compressors) where mass crosses system boundaries at a steady rate:

Steady Flow Conservation Formula

$$\dot{Q} - \dot{W} = \dot{m} \left[ (h_2 - h_1) + \frac{V_2^2 - V_1^2}{2000} + \frac{g(z_2 - z_1)}{1000} \right]$$

Where $h$ is specific enthalpy ($\text{kJ/kg}$), $V$ is fluid velocity ($\text{m/s}$), and $z$ is elevation ($\text{m}$).
