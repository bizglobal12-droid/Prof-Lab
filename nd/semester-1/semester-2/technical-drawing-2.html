MEC 123: Technical Drawing II

Module 1: Machine Assembly Conventions, Mechanical Fasteners, Limits and Fits

1.1 Machine Assembly Drawings & Bill of Materials

Assembly drawings illustrate the spatial arrangement and relative positioning of individual fabricated components within a complete engineering sub-assembly or machine unit.

Essential Components of an Assembly Layout

  • Item Reference Numbers (Balloons): Circled numbers with leader lines pointing directly to each constituent part.
  • Bill of Materials (BOM / Part List): Itemized table listing item number, description, quantity, material specification, and part drawing reference.
  • Sectional Views: Cutaway views revealing interior mechanical features, clearance paths, and component interlocks.
ISO Hatching Conventions for Assemblies

Adjacent mating parts must be hatched at alternating $45^\circ$ angles or varying line spacing. Solid shafts, bolts, nuts, studs, pins, and keys are shown unsectioned when cut longitudinally.

1.2 Limits, Fits, and Engineering Tolerances

To ensure component interchangeability in mass production, dimensional tolerances define acceptable variations from nominal size.

Classification of Engineering Fits (ISO System)

  • Clearance Fit: Always leaves a positive spatial gap between mating shaft and hole (e.g., rotating journal bearings).
  • Transition Fit: Hole and shaft tolerance zones overlap; fit may result in slight clearance or slight interference.
  • Interference Fit: Shaft is larger than hole prior to assembly, requiring thermal expansion or hydraulic press fit (e.g., bush bearings).
$$\text{Fundamental Tolerance} = \text{Upper Limit Size} - \text{Lower Limit Size}$$
