MEC 111: Mechanical Engineering Science I

Module 1: Statics & Concurrent Force Systems

1.1 Introduction to Concurrent Force Systems

In mechanical statics, a force system is categorized by the lines of action of the constituent forces. A concurrent force system consists of two or more forces whose lines of action intersect at a single common point.

Fundamental Principles

  • Scalar Quantity: A physical magnitude without direction (e.g., mass, density).
  • Vector Quantity: A physical property possessing both magnitude and direction (e.g., force, velocity).
  • Line of Action: The infinite line along which a force vector acts.
Resultant Force ($R$) of Two Concurrent Forces

$$R = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2 + 2PQ \cos(\theta)}$$

Where $P$ and $Q$ represent force vectors acting at angle $\theta$.

Conditions for Static Equilibrium

For a rigid body acting under coplanar, concurrent forces to remain in complete equilibrium, the vector sum of all acting forces must equate to zero:

$$\sum F_x = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \sum F_y = 0$$

Graphically, this is verified using the Polygon of Forces rule: if a system of forces in equilibrium is drawn head-to-tail to scale, it forms a closed geometric polygon.
