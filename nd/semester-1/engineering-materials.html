MEC 114: Engineering Materials I

Module 1: Classification, Structure, and Mechanical Properties

1.1 Classification of Engineering Materials

Engineering materials are fundamental substances used in industrial manufacturing, structural fabrication, and machine component design. They are broadly categorized into four primary classes based on atomic structure and bonding:

  • Ferrous Metals: Iron-based metals containing carbon (e.g., plain carbon steels, cast iron, alloy steels).
  • Non-Ferrous Metals: Metals containing no deliberate iron additions (e.g., aluminum, copper, brass, bronze, titanium).
  • Polymers: Organic compounds formed by long-chain molecular monomers (e.g., thermoplastics, thermosets, elastomers).
  • Ceramics & Composites: Inorganic non-metallic solids (ceramics) or combinations of distinct material phases engineered for synergistic performance (composites).

1.2 Key Mechanical Properties

The suitability of a material for a specific structural application is governed by its response to externally applied loads.

Tensile Testing & Hooke's Law

Within the elastic limit of a material, normal stress ($\sigma$) is directly proportional to linear strain ($\epsilon$).

Young's Modulus of Elasticity ($E$)

$$E = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon} = \frac{F / A}{\Delta L / L_0}$$

Where $F$ is applied force (N), $A$ is cross-sectional area ($\text{m}^2$), $\Delta L$ is extension (m), and $L_0$ is original length (m).

$$\text{Ultimate Tensile Strength (UTS)} = \frac{\text{Maximum Force } (F_{\text{max}})}{\text{Original Area } (A_0)}$$
