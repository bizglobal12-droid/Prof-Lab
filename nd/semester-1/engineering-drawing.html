MEC 112: Engineering Drawing I

Module 1: Drawing Standards, Lines, and Layouts

1.1 Standard ISO Lines & Sheet Layouts

Engineering drawing serves as the universal graphical language of technology. To prevent misinterpretation, all technical drawings follow strict international conventions defined by ISO and BS standard specifications.

Standard Line Types & Applications

  • Continuous Thick (Type A): Used for visible outlines and visible edges of components.
  • Continuous Thin (Type B): Used for dimension lines, extension lines, leader lines, and hatching.
  • Dashed Thin (Type E/F): Used for hidden outlines and hidden edges.
  • Chain Thin (Type G): Used for center lines, axes of symmetry, and pitch circles.
Standard Sheet Proportions (ISO A-Series)

$$\text{Aspect Ratio} = 1 : \sqrt{2} \quad \implies \quad \text{Area of } A_0 = 1.0 \, \text{m}^2$$

Every subsequent size ($A_1, A_2, A_3, A_4$) is created by halving the larger dimension of the preceding sheet.

1.2 Orthographic Projection Principles

Orthographic projection is a method of representing a three-dimensional object on two-dimensional plane surfaces using perpendicular projectors.

First-Angle vs. Third-Angle Projection

The primary distinction between standard projection modes lies in the spatial quadrant placement of the object relative to the observer and plane of projection:

$$\text{First-Angle System:} \quad \text{Observer} \longrightarrow \text{Object} \longrightarrow \text{Projection Plane}$$
$$\text{Third-Angle System:} \quad \text{Observer} \longrightarrow \text{Projection Plane} \longrightarrow \text{Object}$$
