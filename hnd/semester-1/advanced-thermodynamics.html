MEC 311: Advanced Thermodynamics & Heat Transfer

Module 1: Vapor Power Cycles and Conduction Heat Transfer

1.1 The Ideal Rankine Vapor Power Cycle

The Rankine cycle is the fundamental thermodynamic model for steam power plants. Unlike the theoretical Carnot cycle, the Rankine cycle avoids superheated compression by condensing steam completely into liquid before pumping.

Thermodynamic Processes in the Rankine Cycle

  • Process 1-2 (Isentropic Compression): Liquid condensate is pumped to boiler pressure ($W_{\text{pump}} = v_1(P_2 - P_1)$).
  • Process 2-3 (Constant Pressure Heat Addition): Water is heated to superheated steam in the boiler ($Q_{\text{in}} = h_3 - h_2$).
  • Process 3-4 (Isentropic Expansion): Steam expands through the turbine to generate work ($W_{\text{turbine}} = h_3 - h_4$).
  • Process 4-1 (Constant Pressure Heat Rejection): Exhaust steam condenses back to liquid in the condenser ($Q_{\text{out}} = h_4 - h_1$).
Rankine Thermal Efficiency ($\eta_{\text{th}}$)

$$\eta_{\text{th}} = \frac{W_{\text{net}}}{Q_{\text{in}}} = \frac{(h_3 - h_4) - (h_2 - h_1)}{h_3 - h_2}$$

Where $h$ represents specific enthalpy ($\text{kJ/kg}$) at each state point across the system.

1.2 Fourier's Law of Heat Conduction

Conduction is the mode of thermal energy transfer through a solid medium via molecular vibration and free electron transport.

$$\dot{Q}_{\text{cond}} = -k A \frac{dT}{dx}$$

Where $k$ is thermal conductivity ($\text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$), $A$ is cross-sectional area ($\text{m}^2$), and $\frac{dT}{dx}$ is the temperature gradient.
