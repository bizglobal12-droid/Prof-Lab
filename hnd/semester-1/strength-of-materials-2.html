MEC 313: Strength of Materials II

Module 1: Thick Cylinders, Unsymmetrical Bending, and Strain Energy Analysis

1.1 Thick Cylinders & Lamé's Equations

A cylinder is classified as thick when its wall thickness ($t$) exceeds $\frac{1}{20}\text{th}$ of its internal diameter ($d_i$). Unlike thin cylinders, radial stress ($\sigma_r$) cannot be neglected and varies non-linearly across the wall thickness alongside hoop stress ($\sigma_c$).

Lamé's Stress Equations

$$\text{Radial Stress: } \sigma_r = \frac{B}{r^2} - A$$

$$\text{Hoop / Circumferential Stress: } \sigma_c = \frac{B}{r^2} + A$$

Where $r$ is the variable radius at any point within the wall thickness, and $A$ and $B$ are Lamé constants determined by boundary pressure conditions at internal ($r_1$) and external ($r_2$) radii.

1.2 Strain Energy & Castigliano's Theorem

Strain energy ($U$) is the internal work done by external forces in deforming an elastic body.

Strain Energy in Direct Axially Loaded Members

$$U = \int \frac{P^2}{2AE} \, dx = \frac{P^2 L}{2AE}$$
Castigliano's First Theorem

$$\delta_i = \frac{\partial U}{\partial P_i}$$

The partial derivative of total strain energy ($U$) with respect to an applied point load ($P_i$) equals the displacement ($\delta_i$) of the point of application along the load's line of action.
