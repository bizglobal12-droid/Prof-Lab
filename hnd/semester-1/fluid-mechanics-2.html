MEC 312: Fluid Mechanics II & Turbo-Machinery

Module 1: Boundary Layer Behavior, Dimensional Analysis, and Hydrodynamics

1.1 Boundary Layer Concept & Viscous Drag

When a real fluid flows past a solid boundary, no-slip boundary conditions dictate that fluid particles adhering to the wall remain stationary. A thin region adjacent to the surface develops where velocity gradients and shear forces predominate: the boundary layer ($\delta$).

Laminar to Turbulent Transition

Flow regime stability over a flat plate is governed by the local Reynolds number ($Re_x$). Transition typically initiates at critical Reynolds numbers:

$$Re_{x,\text{crit}} = \frac{\rho U_{\infty} x}{\mu} \approx 5 \times 10^5$$
Prandtl Boundary Layer Thickness (Laminar Flow)

$$\delta \approx \frac{5.0 x}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$$

Where $x$ is the distance from the leading edge and $Re_x$ is the local Reynolds number.

1.2 Euler's Turbo-Machinery Equation

Euler's turbine equation represents the fundamental angular momentum balance for all dynamic fluid machinery, linking fluid velocity vectors at impeller inlet and outlet to shaft torque and power.

Specific Work Output / Input ($w$)

$$w = U_1 V_{w1} - U_2 V_{w2}$$

Where $U$ is blade tangential speed ($\text{m/s}$) and $V_w$ is the whirl component of fluid velocity ($\text{m/s}$) at inlet (1) and exit (2).
